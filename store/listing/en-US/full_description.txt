Prosoche is the Stoic practice of attention: noticing, again and again, where your mind is going. This app gives you a short daily set of texts from the Stoics instead of an endless feed.

A DAILY SET THAT ENDS
Each day you get a small set of cards: short quotes, longer passages, a question to reflect on, a Stoic idea or an exercise. When the set is done, it is done. There is nothing more to scroll.

READ THE WHOLE WORKS
The Library has the complete texts, offline:
- Marcus Aurelius, Meditations (translated by George Long, 1862)
- Epictetus, Discourses and Enchiridion (translated by George Long, 1877)
- Seneca, Of the Shortness of Life, Of Anger, Of Peace of Mind, Of a Happy Life and his other minor dialogues (translated by Aubrey Stewart, 1889)
Your reading position is saved. Bookmark sections, search the texts and choose a comfortable reading size. Every quote on a card can be opened in its full context.

PRACTICE
- Morning preparation, after Meditations 2.1
- Evening review, after Seneca, Of Anger 3.36
- A private journal for your answers
- Memento mori: your life in weeks
- Short exercises such as the view from above, sorting what is yours, and a digital fast

JOURNEY
Your streak, with grace days for the days that do not go to plan, and a calendar of the days you showed up.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
- No account. No ads.
- Your journal, saved cards and progress are stored on your phone. We do not receive them.
- Anonymous usage statistics and crash reports are off by default and only sent if you turn them on.
- Reminders are optional: a morning reminder, and an evening reflection reminder that is off unless you turn it on.

The texts are public domain translations, quoted word for word with the work and section. Sources and licenses are listed in the app.
